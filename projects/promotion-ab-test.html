Choosing the Best of Three Promotions with an A/B/N Test

A fast-food chain tested three promotions for a new menu item, each in a random set of stores for four weeks. I analysed the results to decide which promotion to roll out.

137stores in 10 markets, 4 weeks each
−10kweekly sales per store, Promotion 2 vs 1
+9%Promotion 1 over Promotion 3, within markets

Step 1: check the randomisation

Before comparing results, I checked that the three groups were comparable. Store age (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.56) and market size (chi-square p = 0.88) were balanced across promotions. Stores were not spread evenly across the 10 markets, though: one market had no stores at all for Promotion 2. That detail becomes important in step 3.

Step 2: one promotion clearly underperforms

Each store appears four times, so treating 548 rows as independent would overstate precision. I averaged each store's four weeks and compared the 137 store averages with ANOVA and Tukey's test.

Step 3: compare stores within the same market

Market explains most of the variation in sales: stores in large markets sell far more than stores in small ones. Because the promotions were unevenly spread across markets, that noise hides smaller differences. Adding market fixed effects to the regression compares each store only with stores in its own market, with standard errors clustered by store.

Step 4: how sensitive was the test?

With about 46 stores per promotion, a simple two-group comparison could only reliably detect differences of around 9.6k per week (80% power, 5% significance), about 18% of average sales. That explains why the raw 1-vs-3 gap was invisible, and why reducing noise was the key analytical step.

Step 5: stress-test the result

Market size

The ranking holds in small, medium and large markets (interaction p = 0.11).

Store age

Promotion 1's lead over 3 shrinks from about 6.5k in new stores to under 3k in 20-year-old stores, but it wins at every age. Worth testing directly next time.

Unusual stores

Three stores sit far from the model's prediction; removing them barely changes the estimates. Group variances are similar (Levene p = 0.63).

Recommendation

  1. Drop Promotion 2. It was the weakest in every analysis and every market size.
  2. Roll out Promotion 1, about 9% ahead of Promotion 3 once stores are compared within their market.
  3. Check costs first. The data has no promotion costs; a much cheaper Promotion 3 could still win on profit.
  4. Next time, stratify: randomise within each market so every market tests all three promotions, check whether Promotion 1's lead really is smaller in older stores, and run longer than four weeks.

About this project

Personal project in Python (pandas, statsmodels, SciPy, matplotlib), built to put the A/B/N test design from my Marketing Analytics module into practice on a public test dataset.

Data: public A/B test sample dataset (IBM Watson Analytics, via Kaggle). The promotions are not described in the data, and there is no cost or profit information.
